\begin{frame}{Thresholds from a history}
  \begingroup\centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{images/day13/threshold.pdf}\par
  \endgroup
  \vskip 0.1em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Record a reference period with no problem (here one hour). Take
          the mean minus 3 standard deviations of the windows.
    \item 10 s means: 0.769 $\pm$ 0.026, threshold 0.689. 60 s means: 0.769
          $\pm$ 0.012, threshold 0.733.
    \item A longer window has less noise, so its threshold can be closer to
          the normal value. It sees a smaller drift, but later.
    \item When the values are not normal, use a percentile (for example
          the 0.1 percent point).
  \end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ false
\begin{frame}{How many false alerts?}
  \small
  \[ P(\text{at least one false alert}) = 1 - (1 - \alpha)^{N} \]
  \vskip 0.3em
  \footnotesize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Rule: mean $-$ 3 sd, one day} & \textbf{Checks} &
      \textbf{False alerts} & \textbf{At least one} \\
    \midrule
    Check each 10 s & 8640 & 11.7 & 100\,\% \\
    Check each minute & 1440 & 1.9 & 86\,\% \\
    20 metrics, each minute & 28\,800 & 38.9 & \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.6em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\alpha$: the chance that one check of a normal value passes the
          threshold (0.00135 for 3 sd on one side). $N$: the number of
          checks.
    \item Many checks make false alerts certain. After some days of false
          alerts, people do not read them any more.
    \item Fewer, better rules: a longer window, a duration, and a rule for
          each cause, not for each metric.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{Formula: \emph{Machine Learning Systems}, Volume II, chapter ``ML
    Operations at Scale'' (alert fatigue). Values: normal distribution,
    independent checks.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ psi
\begin{frame}{PSI: compare two distributions}
  \small
  \[ \text{PSI} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (a_i - e_i)\,\ln\frac{a_i}{e_i} \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Divide the range of the value into $n$ fixed bins (here: 10 bins
          of the confidence, 0 to 1). $e_i$: the part of the reference in
          bin $i$. $a_i$: the part of the current window.
    \item A bin with no value makes the logarithm infinite. Give each empty
          bin a small part (here 0.0001).
    \item Common limits: below 0.1 stable, 0.1 to 0.25 a warning, above 0.25
          a large change. They are conventions, not laws.
    \item In the simulation, the PSI of 10 min against the reference hour
          stayed below 0.017 in the normal hours, also with the short dark
          event. After one hour of the gradual drift it was 1.77.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{Formula and limits: \emph{Machine Learning Systems}, Volume I,
    chapter ``ML Operations''. Values: simulation of this part.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ lessons
\begin{frame}{What the comparison shows}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A short window is fast and noisy}: 4 false alerts in 5
          hours, and it misses the small drift (light x 0.5).
    \item \textbf{A duration removes the false alerts}, also the short event,
          but it adds delay and misses the small drift.
    \item \textbf{A longer window} sees the small drift in 40 s with 1 false
          alert: the short event, which is a real change of 30 s.
    \item \textbf{The brightness} sees each change of the light in 10 s,
          and it does not see the blur. It names the cause.
    \item \textbf{The PSI} gave no false alert. It needed 8 to 22 minutes
          and missed the small drift: a check for a daily report.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{Alert on one stable model rule (a longer window with a
    duration). Show the input statistics next to it to find the cause.}
  \source{Simulation of this part. The frames are independent; a real
    camera gives correlated frames and more noise between windows.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{After the alert}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \small
      \textbf{Diagnose}
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item Look at the device: camera, light, lens, position.
        \item System metrics: temperature, memory, restarts.
        \item Input statistics: which one changed?
        \item Changes: a new model, a new program, a new place.
        \item Compare with the same hour of an earlier day.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \small
      \textbf{Incident report}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Time of the start, of the alert, of the end
        \item How you found it: rule, value, threshold
        \item Effect: what was wrong, for how long
        \item Cause and action
        \item What changes so that it does not happen again
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.6em
  \small
  The lab hands in a short incident report for the drift event of Part C.
  The report is a record of what happened, not a search for a person to
  blame.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Not every alert needs a new model}
  \footnotesize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begingroup\centering
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.25\textwidth}L{0.33\textwidth}L{0.32\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Cause} & \textbf{Signal} & \textbf{Action} \\
    \midrule
    Sensor fault & Failed input checks; a stuck or black frame & Repair or
      clean; no training \\
    Training-serving skew & Low confidence at once after a release & Fix the
      code; roll back \\
    Input drift ($P(x)$) & Input statistics and confidence change & New
      data of the new scene; retrain \\
    Prior drift ($P(y)$) & Class counts change, confidence stays & Often
      nothing; check the thresholds \\
    Concept drift ($P(y \mid x)$) & Only labels show it & New labels;
      retrain \\
    Heat, leak & System metrics & Cooling, a fix of the code \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\par\endgroup
  \vskip 0.6em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Find which part changed before you retrain. A model trained on the
          data of a broken sensor learns the fault.
    \item The first two rows are the most frequent and the cheapest to fix.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{\emph{Machine Learning Systems}, Volume I, chapter ``ML
    Operations'' (diagnose the drift before retraining).}
\end{frame}

\exerciseframe{Set alert thresholds}{%
  \small
  A reference hour of the stream of this part gives these 60 s means of the
  confidence, one each 5 minutes:
  \begin{center}
    0.782, 0.782, 0.764, 0.780, 0.762, 0.771, 0.764, 0.787, 0.780, 0.775,

  \end{center}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[A.] Calculate the mean and the standard deviation. Set a threshold
          at the mean minus 3 standard deviations.
    \item[B.] All 355 windows of the same hour give 0.769 $\pm$ 0.012. Which
          threshold is better, and why?
    \item[C.] The Raspberry Pi throttles at 80\,$^\circ$C. Set a warning and
          a critical rule for the temperature, each with a duration.
  \end{enumerate}}

\answerframe{Set alert thresholds}{%
  \small
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[A.] Mean 0.774, standard deviation 0.0085, threshold
          $0.774 - 3 \times 0.0085 = 0.748$.
    \item[B.] 12 values are too few: they give a small standard deviation by
          chance. 5 percent of the 355 windows of the reference hour are
          below 0.748: with one check each minute, about 73 checks each day
          fire with no problem. The threshold of all windows is
          $0.769 - 3 \times 0.012 = 0.733$. Use a long history, and check how
          often the threshold fires in it.
    \item[C.] The temperature has a limit from the hardware, so it needs no
          history. For example: a warning above 75\,$^\circ$C for 5 minutes
          (the cooler is at full speed); a critical alert above
          80\,$^\circ$C for 30 s (throttling, the rule of HW-08).
  \end{enumerate}}
