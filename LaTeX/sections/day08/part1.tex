  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Two stages:} R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, Faster R-CNN. The second
          stage runs for each proposed region. This costs time.
    \item \textbf{One stage:} SSD, EfficientDet, YOLO, and FOMO. One pass

    \item The cost of a single-stage model does not depend on the number of

  \end{itemize}

    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item The last map of the backbone has $20 \times 20$ cells for an
              image of $640 \times 640$ pixels. One cell covers
              $32 \times 32$ pixels: the \textbf{stride} is 32.
        \item The head gives one vector for each cell: 4 box values and

    numbers for the box.}

        \draw[coolgray, dashed, thick] (2.5,2.5) rectangle (6.5,6.5);
        \draw[coolgray, dashed, thick] (1.7,3.1) rectangle (7.3,5.9);
        \draw[coolgray, dashed, thick] (3.1,1.7) rectangle (5.9,7.3);
        \draw[darkcyan, very thick] (2.2,3.4) rectangle (8.6,6.4);
        \draw[->, darkcyan!70!black] (7.3,5.9) -- (8.5,6.35);
        \node[coolgray, above] at (5,9) {dashed: 3 default boxes};
        \node[darkcyan!70!black, below] at (5,0)
          {solid: the predicted box};
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.56\textwidth}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item An \textbf{anchor} is a default box with a fixed size and a
              fixed shape. Each cell has $K$ anchors.
        \item For each anchor, the head predicts 4 corrections and the
              class scores:
              \[
                K \times (4 + \text{classes}) \ \text{values for a cell}
              \]
        \item The corrections move the anchor and change its size.
        \item The designer selects the sizes and the shapes of the anchors
              for the objects of the dataset.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.3em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item SSD and EfficientDet use anchors. YOLOv8 and the newer YOLO
          models use no anchors: each cell predicts the distances to the
          four sides of the box.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{With anchors, the number of candidates is the number of cells
    times $K$.}
\end{frame}

  \end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{The head of YOLO11: no anchors}
  \begingroup\centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\scriptsize, >={Stealth[length=4pt]},
      b/.style={draw=coolgray, rounded corners=3pt, fill=boxgray,
                minimum width=2.3cm, minimum height=0.85cm, align=center}]
    \node[b] (a) at (0,0) {One cell\\of one map};
    \node[b, draw=darkcyan]    (b) at (3.1,0.65)
      {Box branch:\\$4 \times 16$ values};
    \node[b, draw=cardinalred] (c) at (3.1,-0.65)
      {Class branch:\\80 values};
    \node[b, draw=darkcyan]    (d) at (6.2,0.65)
      {4 distances to the\\sides of the box};
    \node[b, draw=cardinalred] (e) at (6.2,-0.65)
      {80 scores,\\one sigmoid each};
    \draw[->] (a.east) -- (b.west);
    \draw[->] (a.east) -- (c.west);
    \draw[->] (b) -- (d);
    \draw[->] (c) -- (e);
    \node[draw=treegreen, rounded corners=3pt, fill=boxgray, align=center,
          minimum height=2.15cm] (f) at (8.75,0) {84\\values};
    \draw[->] (d.east) -- (d.east -| f.west);
    \draw[->] (e.east) -- (e.east -| f.west);
  \end{tikzpicture}\par\endgroup
  \vskip 0.4em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Box:} for each of the four sides, 16 values give a
          probability for each distance from 0 to 15 cells. The mean is the
          distance. The program converts the distances to
          $c_x, c_y, w, h$.
    \item \textbf{Class:} each class has its own sigmoid. The 80 scores of
          a cell do not add up to 1.
    \item The head has no separate score for ``an object is here''. The
          score of a candidate is its largest class score.
    \item YOLOv8 has the same head. YOLO26 predicts each distance with one
          value.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{The head of the Ultralytics models, read from the package. The
    raw outputs have 64 and 80 channels for each cell.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------- raw output
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Read the raw output}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=python]
from ultralytics import YOLO

model = YOLO("yolo11n.pt")
raw = model.model(image)[0][0]      # image: 1 x 3 x 640 x 640, values 0 to 1
print(raw.shape)                    # torch.Size([84, 8400])

boxes  = raw[:4].T                  # 8400 rows: cx, cy, w, h in pixels
scores = raw[4:].max(0).values      # the largest class score of each cell
labels = raw[4:].argmax(0)          # the class with this score
keep   = scores > 0.25
print(int(keep.sum()))              # 47
\end{lstlisting}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The output has 84 rows and 8400 columns: 705\,600 numbers. The
          80 classes are the classes of the dataset COCO.
    \item For the test image, 47 cells have a score above 0.25. The image
          shows 5 objects.
    \item Normally, \code{model.predict()} does these steps and the
          post-processing for you.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The output of the network is not a list of objects. It is one
    candidate for each cell.}
\end{frame}

% -------------------------------------------------------- how many candidates
\begin{frame}{How many candidates?}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.25\textwidth}rrrL{0.26\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Image} & \textbf{Maps} & \textbf{Candidates}
    & \textbf{Values for each} \\
    \midrule
    SSD MobileNet V1   & 300 & 6 & 1917     & $4 + 91$ \\
    EfficientDet-Lite0 & 320 & 5 & 19\,206  & $4 + 90$ \\
    YOLO11n            & 320 & 3 & 2100     & $4 + 80$ \\
    YOLO11n            & 640 & 3 & 8400     & $4 + 80$ \\
    FOMO               & 160 & 1 & 400      & 3, and no box \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The number of candidates is fixed. It comes from the image size,
          the strides, and the anchors.
    \item An empty image and a full image give the same number.
    \item Two times the image side gives four times the candidates.
    \item The head of FOMO gives $20 \times 20$ cells with three classes:
          the background and two objects.
  \end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{IoU: the overlap of two boxes}
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \centering
      \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\scriptsize, x=0.55cm, y=0.55cm]
        \fill[darkcyan!18] (0,1) rectangle (4,5);
        \fill[darkcyan!18] (2,0) rectangle (6,4);
        \fill[cardinalred!55] (2,1) rectangle (4,4);
        \draw[darkcyan, thick] (0,1) rectangle (4,5);
        \draw[darkcyan, thick] (2,0) rectangle (6,4);
        \node at (1,4.3) {A};
        \node at (5,0.7) {B};
        \node[white] at (3,2.5) {\textbf{I}};
      \end{tikzpicture}
      \[
        \text{IoU} = \frac{\text{area of the intersection}}
                          {\text{area of the union}}
      \]
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item IoU: ``intersection over union''.
        \item The intersection is the area that the two boxes share. The
              union is the area that one box or the two boxes cover.
        \item Here A and B have $4 \times 4 = 16$ squares each. The
              intersection has $2 \times 3 = 6$ squares.
        \item The union has $16 + 16 - 6 = 26$ squares. The IoU is
              $6 / 26 = 0.23$.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.4em
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.30\textwidth}L{0.30\textwidth}L{0.30\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{IoU = 0} & \textbf{IoU = 0.5} & \textbf{IoU = 1} \\
    \midrule
    The boxes do not touch. & Two equal boxes share two thirds of their
    area. & The boxes are the same. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.3em
  \keypoint{Two candidates with a high IoU show the same object. Part 2
    uses the IoU to compare a prediction with a label.}
\end{frame}

% --------------------------------------------------------------------- NMS
\begin{frame}{Step 2: non-maximum suppression}
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \begin{column}{0.60\textwidth}
      \small
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item Remove the candidates below the score threshold. Sort the
              other candidates by their score.
        \item Take the candidate with the highest score. \textbf{Keep it.}
        \item Calculate its IoU with each other candidate of the same
              class.
        \item \textbf{Remove} each candidate with an IoU above the IoU
              threshold.
        \item Do steps 2 to 4 again with the candidates that are left,
              until no candidate is left.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.38\textwidth}
      \centering
      \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\scriptsize, x=0.36cm, y=0.36cm]
        \fill[boxgray] (0,0) rectangle (10,7);
        \draw[cardinalred, very thick] (1,1) rectangle (5,6);
        \draw[coolgray, dashed] (1.5,0.6) rectangle (5.6,5.6);
        \draw[coolgray, dashed] (0.6,1.5) rectangle (4.6,6.4);
        \draw[cardinalred, very thick] (6.5,2) rectangle (9.3,5.2);
        \draw[coolgray, dashed] (6.9,1.6) rectangle (9.6,4.8);
        \node[cardinalred] at (3,3.5) {0.9};
        \node[cardinalred] at (7.9,3.6) {0.8};
        \node[below, align=center] at (5,0)
          {solid: kept\\dashed: removed};
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.4em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The method is greedy. It never looks at a decision again.
    \item It has no weights. It is normal code after the network.
    \item For $N$ candidates, it calculates up to $N^2 / 2$ values of the
          IoU.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The suppression keeps the best box of each group.}
\end{frame}

\answerframe{suppression of six boxes by hand}{%
  \footnotesize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrL{0.30\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Pair} & \textbf{Intersection} & \textbf{Union} & \textbf{IoU}
    & \textbf{With the threshold 0.5} \\
    \midrule
    A and B & $3 \times 6 = 18$ & $24 + 24 - 18 = 30$ & 0.60
            & A removes B \\
    A and C & $2 \times 6 = 12$ & $24 + 24 - 12 = 36$ & 0.33
            & C stays \\
    D and E & $3 \times 3 = 9$  & $12 + 12 - 9 = 15$  & 0.60
            & D removes E \\
    D and F & $3 \times 3 = 9$  & $12 + 9 - 9 = 12$   & 0.75
            & Different classes \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.3em
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The order is A, D, B, E, C for the persons. A stays and removes
          B. D stays and removes E. C stays: its IoU with A is 0.33, and
          nobody compares it with B, because B is not in the list now. F is
          the only dog. \textbf{Result: A, C, D, F.}
    \item The IoU of A and C is 0.33, above 0.3. A removes C also.
          \textbf{Result: A, D, F.}
    \item D removes F: the IoU is 0.75. \textbf{Result: A, C, D.}
  \end{enumerate}
  \vskip 0.2em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item C is a second person next to the first person, or a third box of
          the same person. The suppression cannot know this. The IoU
          threshold decides.
    \item F is a dog in front of a person, or a wrong class for the
          person. With one class for each object in your application, use
          the suppression for all classes.
  \end{itemize}
}
