% ------------------------------------------------------------------ why
\begin{frame}{Design before you build}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item On Days 1 to 13 each lab gave you the task, the boards, and the
          model. In a project you select them.
    \item A change on paper costs minutes. The same change after the build
          costs days. After the installation of 100 devices, it needs an
          update of each device (Part 1).
    \item Most failures of a deployed system come from a requirement that
          nobody wrote down: the battery life, the light at night, the
          network of the site.
    \item A design gives the team one plan with numbers. A review finds the
          weak points before the build.
  \end{itemize}
  \vskip 0.4em
  \begin{block}{This part}
    A method in seven steps, applied to one example. Then the budgets for
    power and cost, the design review, and the capstone project of Days 14
    and 15.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ problem
\begin{frame}{Step 1: the problem and the user}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Four questions}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{For whom?} The user and the people near the device.
          \item \textbf{Where?} The place, the power, the network, the light,
                the temperature.
          \item \textbf{What?} The function that solves the problem.
          \item \textbf{Which benefit?} What is better than today?
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Example of this part}
        A workshop has 20 machines. A bearing that fails makes the machine
        vibrate. Today a technician hears it, often too late. The owner
        wants an alarm at the machine and a dashboard for the technician.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Write the problem, not a solution: ``detect a failing bearing'',
          not ``run YOLO on a camera''.
    \item Start with a minimum viable product: the smallest system that
          shows that the idea works.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Power budget: the XIAO on a battery}
  \footnotesize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.46\textwidth}rr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Design (battery 1000 mAh, 3.7 V: 3700 mWh)} & \textbf{Mean
      power} & \textbf{Life} \\
    \midrule
    Wi-Fi always connected (Sense board in modem sleep) & at least 167.2 mW
      & at most 22 h \\
    Model 50 ms for each 2 s window, light sleep between, Wi-Fi 3 s each
      hour & 24.2 mW & 6.4 days \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.6em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Mean power: $P = d \cdot P_\text{work} + (1 - d) \cdot
          P_\text{rest}$ with the duty cycle $d = 50 / 2000 = 2.5$ percent
          (Day 9).
    \item In the second design, the light sleep of the Sense board (19 mW)
          is 77 percent of the energy. The model is not the problem.
    \item 30 days need 17.4 Wh: a battery of about 4700 mAh. Or change R6:
          a cable from a USB supply at the machine.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{Day 9 values (ESP32-S3 data sheet 2.2; Seeed board values).}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ review
\begin{frame}{Design review: what the reviewer checks}
  \small
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Each requirement has a number and a test.
    \item Each budget has a value and a source: latency, memory, power,
          cost. A guess is marked as a guess.
    \item The model fits the board: flash, RAM, time.
    \item The data: where it comes from, how many samples, who labels it,
          and a test set that the training does not see.
    \item The failure modes: no network, a failed sensor, a bad update
          (Parts 1 and 2).
    \item The threats and the data that leaves the device (Part 2).
    \item The riskiest assumption and its first test.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vskip 0.4em
  The reviewer asks questions; the team answers with numbers. A weak point
  is a result of the review, not a failure of the team.
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ capstone 1
\begin{frame}{Capstone: the five requirements}
  \small
  Your system must:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Use the XIAOML Kit and the Raspberry Pi.
    \item Run at least one optimized model, with the size and the accuracy
          before and after the optimization.
    \item Make one decision locally, and continue to work without the
          internet.
    \item Send telemetry with MQTT to a dashboard.
    \item Include a benchmark table with latency, memory, and energy.

\exerciseframe{Review a parking gate design}{%
  A team proposes this design:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A XIAOML Kit on a pole at the parking entrance, on a battery of
          1000 mAh. Wi-Fi stays connected.
    \item Each second, it sends a camera image of 96 x 96 pixels to a cloud
          server. YOLO11n at 640 pixels detects cars there.
    \item The cloud sends ``open'' to the gate when it detects a car.
    \item Accuracy: 99 percent on the training images.
    \item The team updates the model with a USB cable. The broker has no
          password.
  \end{itemize}
  Find three weak points. For each one, give a number or a result of the
  course, and propose a change.}

\answerframe{Review a parking gate design}{%
  \footnotesize
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Power}: the Sense board alone needs at least 167.2 mW
          with Wi-Fi connected: at most 22 h on 1000 mAh, before the camera.
          Change: mains power, or a local model and light sleep (Day 9).
    \item \textbf{No internet, no gate}: the decision is in the cloud, so the
          gate fails with the link (requirement 3). Change: detect on the
          device, send only events (Day 12).
    \item \textbf{Accuracy on training images}: Day 8 gave 0.670 on training
          images and 0.546 on test images. Change: a test set of the site.
    \item \textbf{Model and images}: YOLO11n at 640 pixels on an image of
          96 pixels gives no detail; the images can show persons and number
          plates (privacy, Part 2).
    \item \textbf{Updates and broker}: a USB cable on a pole does not scale
          (Part 1); an open broker accepts a false ``open'' (Part 2).
  \end{itemize}}
