        \draw[->] (i) -- (l); \draw[->] (l) -- (o);

      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item When a layer runs, its input and its output are in the RAM.
        \item After the layer, the runtime can use the input buffer again.
        \item The peak is the largest sum of an input and an output, for all
              layers.
        \item A skip connection keeps one more tensor.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item In \code{int8}, one value is one byte. So the peak in values is
          the peak in bytes.
    \item The arena also holds the data of the interpreter. For a vision
          model, this part is small against the activations.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The size of the model file says nothing about the peak. You
    must look at the feature maps.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{MobileNet: the depthwise separable convolution}
  \begingroup\centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\footnotesize, >={Stealth[length=5pt]},
      b/.style={draw=coolgray, rounded corners=3pt, fill=boxgray,
                minimum width=2.3cm, minimum height=0.95cm, align=center},
      lab/.style={font=\scriptsize, text=coolgray, align=center}]
    \node[b] (s) at (0,0) {Convolution\\$3 \times 3$};
    \node[lab] at (0,-0.85) {$9 \cdot C_{in} \cdot C_{out}$ weights};
    \node at (2.1,0) {becomes};
    \node[b, draw=cardinalred] (d) at (4.4,0) {Depthwise\\$3 \times 3$};
    \node[b, draw=darkcyan] (p) at (7.6,0) {Pointwise\\$1 \times 1$};
    \draw[->] (d) -- (p);
    \node[lab] at (4.4,-0.85) {one filter for each\\channel: $9 \cdot C_{in}$};
    \node[lab] at (7.6,-0.85) {mixes the channels:\\$C_{in} \cdot C_{out}$};
  \end{tikzpicture}\par\endgroup
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A normal convolution filters the space and mixes the channels in
          one step.
    \item MobileNet does the two jobs in two layers. Example with 48 input
          channels and 48 output channels: 20\,736 weights become
          $432 + 2304 = 2736$ weights.
    \item The number of operations decreases by the same factor: about 8.
    \item The feature maps do not become smaller. The layer saves flash and
          time, but it saves no RAM.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{MobileNet solves the flash budget and the time budget. The RAM
    budget needs two more controls.}
\end{frame}

  \begingroup\centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\footnotesize, >={Stealth[length=5pt]},
      b/.style={draw=coolgray, rounded corners=3pt, fill=boxgray,
                minimum width=2.0cm, align=center},

      {Expand\\$1 \times 1$};
    \node[b, draw=cardinalred, minimum height=1.9cm] (d) at (5.4,0)
      {Depthwise\\$3 \times 3$};
    \node[b, minimum height=0.6cm] (p) at (8.1,0) {Project\\$1 \times 1$};
    \draw[->] (i) -- (e); \draw[->] (e) -- (d); \draw[->] (d) -- (p);
    \node[lab] at (0,-1.3)   {8 channels};
    \node[lab] at (2.7,-1.3) {48 channels};
    \node[lab] at (5.4,-1.3) {48 channels};
    \node[lab] at (8.1,-1.3) {8 channels};
    \draw[->, coolgray, dashed] (i.north) -- ++(0,0.9) -| node[pos=0.25,
      above, font=\scriptsize] {skip connection, when the sizes are equal}
      (p.north);
  \end{tikzpicture}\par\endgroup
  \vskip 0.3em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The block has a narrow input and a narrow output. Inside, the
          expand layer makes 6 times as many channels.
    \item The depthwise layer works on the wide tensor. This is the place
          of the peak.
    \item The narrow tensors between the blocks keep the skip connection
          small.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{The channel numbers are the numbers of the second block for a
    width multiplier of 0.35. Block: Sandler et al., ``MobileNetV2: Inverted
    Residuals and Linear Bottlenecks'', 2018.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Control 1: the input resolution}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.17\textwidth}rrrr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Image} & \textbf{Parameters} & \textbf{MACs}
    & \textbf{Peak in \code{int8}} & \textbf{Against 96} \\
    \midrule
    $96 \times 96$   & 410\,208 & 10.7 million & 135 KB & 1.0 \\
    $160 \times 160$ & 410\,208 & 29.6 million & 375 KB & 2.8 \\
    $224 \times 224$ & 410\,208 & 58.0 million & 735 KB & 5.4 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.6em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The table shows MobileNetV2 with a width multiplier of 0.35, with
          no classification layer.
    \item The filters do not change with the image size. So the number of
          parameters and the flash use stay equal.
    \item Each feature map grows with the area of the image. The peak and
          the operations grow with the square of the side:
          $(160 / 96)^2 = 2.8$.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The resolution is the strongest control for the RAM and for the
    time. It costs accuracy when the object becomes too small in the image.}
  \source{A calculation with the Keras model of MobileNetV2.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Control 2: the width multiplier}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.24\textwidth}rrr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Width multiplier $\alpha$} & \textbf{Parameters}
    & \textbf{MACs} & \textbf{Peak in \code{int8}} \\
    \midrule
    0.35 & 410\,208   & 10.7 million & 135 KB \\
    0.50 & 706\,224   & 17.6 million & 135 KB \\
    1.00 & 2\,257\,984 & 55.0 million & 270 KB \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.6em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The table shows MobileNetV2 for an image of $96 \times 96$.
    \item $\alpha$ multiplies the number of channels of each layer. The
          result is rounded to a multiple of 8.
    \item A layer has $C_{in} \cdot C_{out}$ weights. So the parameters and
          the operations grow about with $\alpha^2$.
    \item A feature map grows with $\alpha$. The values 0.35 and 0.50 give
          the same first layers after the rounding, so the peak is equal.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The width is the control for the flash. The resolution is the
    control for the RAM. The two change the time.}
  \source{A calculation with the Keras model of MobileNetV2.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Visual wake words}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item The question: is a person in the image, or not?
        \item It is the vision task with the same role as keyword
              spotting: a small model that is always on and wakes a larger
              system.
        \item The dataset comes from a large public dataset of photos.
              Each image has the label ``person'' or ``not person''.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{The target of the paper}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item A microcontroller with about 250 KB of RAM.
          \item Mobile models reach an accuracy of 85 to 90 percent in
                this limit.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A task with two classes needs less from the model than a task
          with 1000 classes. So a small width and a small image are
          enough.
    \item Uses: a light that follows the people in a room, a doorbell, a
          camera that records only with a person in the image.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{Chowdhery et al., ``Visual Wake Words Dataset'', 2019.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The PSRAM: more RAM, at a price}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.22\textwidth}L{0.33\textwidth}L{0.35\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{Internal RAM} & \textbf{PSRAM} \\
    \midrule
    Size      & 512 KB, about 300 KB free & 8 MB \\
    Place     & In the chip & A second chip in the module \\
    Access    & Direct & Through a serial bus: slower \\
    Use it for & The arena of a small model & The image buffers, the arena
                 of a large model \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.6em
  \vskip 0.3em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item One image of $320 \times 240$ with 3 bytes for each pixel needs
          230\,400 bytes. The camera needs the PSRAM for its frame buffer.
    \item A model with a peak above 300 KB runs only with the arena in the
          PSRAM. You measure the price in time in the lab.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The PSRAM removes the RAM limit of this board. A product with a
    smaller chip has no PSRAM.}
  \source{Memory of the board: Day 1 and Day 2 of this course.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Transfer learning: the idea}
  \begingroup\centering

    \node[b, draw=cardinalred, minimum width=2.0cm] (h) at (7.6,0)
      {New last\\layers};
    \draw[->] (i) -- (b); \draw[->] (b) -- (h);

  \end{tikzpicture}\par\endgroup
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A model for images needs thousands of images for each class. You
          have about 50.
    \item The first layers of a CNN learn edges, colours, and textures.
          These features are good for all photos.
    \item So you take a model that learned them from a large dataset. You
          train only the last layers for your classes.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{With transfer learning, 50 images for each class can be
    enough, and the training needs minutes.}
  \source{Method and number of images: kit lab ``Image Classification'' of
    \emph{Machine Learning Systems}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Crop and resize on the device}
  \begingroup\centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\scriptsize, >={Stealth[length=5pt]}]
    % Frame of the camera, 4:3.
    \draw[coolgray, fill=boxgray] (0,0) rectangle (3.2,2.4);
    \draw[cardinalred, line width=1pt] (0.4,0) rectangle (2.8,2.4);
    \node at (1.6,1.2) {$240 \times 240$};
    \node[below, coolgray] at (1.6,0) {camera: $320 \times 240$};
    \draw[->] (3.5,1.2) -- (4.7,1.2) node[midway, above] {resize};
    \draw[cardinalred, line width=1pt, fill=boxgray] (5.0,0.6)
      rectangle (6.2,1.8);
    \node at (5.6,1.2) {$96 \times 96$};
    \node[below, coolgray] at (5.6,0.6) {model};
  \end{tikzpicture}\par\endgroup
  \vskip 0.4em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The camera image is wider than high. The model image is a
          square. In the resize step, each output pixel takes the value of
          the nearest input pixel, or a mean of its neighbours.
    \item \textbf{Crop:} cut the sides. The objects keep their form, and
          the sides of the scene are lost.
    \item \textbf{Squash:} resize the two axes with different factors.
          The complete scene stays, and the objects become narrow.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The object must still be visible in $96 \times 96$ pixels. Look
    at the small image before you train the model.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The limits of a classifier}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What it gives}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item One class for the complete image.
          \item A probability for each class.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What it does not give}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item The position of the object.
          \item The number of objects.
          \item An answer ``none of my classes'', if no class for it
                exists.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Add a class ``background'' with images of the empty scene. It has
          the role of the classes UNKNOWN and NOISE of Part 1.
    \item One object must fill a large part of the image. Two objects of
          different classes in one image give an unclear result.
    \item For the position and the number of objects, you need a detection
          model. Part 3 gives one that fits a microcontroller.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{A classifier answers one question for one image. Design the
    scene of the camera so that this question has a clear answer.}
\end{frame}

\exerciseframe{the peak activation memory}{%
  \small
  The first layers of MobileNetV2 with $\alpha = 0.35$, for an image of
  $96 \times 96$, in \code{int8}. No layer of the table has a skip
  connection.
  \vskip 0.1em
  {\footnotesize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.0}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Input} & \textbf{Output} \\
    \midrule
    1 & Convolution, stride 2 & $96 \times 96 \times 3$
      & $48 \times 48 \times 16$ \\
    2 & Depthwise             & $48 \times 48 \times 16$
      & $48 \times 48 \times 16$ \\
    3 & Project               & $48 \times 48 \times 16$
      & $48 \times 48 \times 8$ \\
    4 & Expand                & $48 \times 48 \times 8$
      & $48 \times 48 \times 48$ \\
    5 & Depthwise, stride 2   & $48 \times 48 \times 48$
      & $24 \times 24 \times 48$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}
  \vskip 0.2em
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Calculate the sum of the input and the output for each layer.
          Which layer has the peak? Give the peak in KB.
    \item The image is now $160 \times 160$. Calculate the new peak with
          the factor between the two image areas.
    \item The board has about 300 KB of free internal RAM. Which model
          runs with no PSRAM?
    \item The frame buffer of the camera needs 230\,400 bytes. Where do you
          put it?
  \end{enumerate}
}

\answerframe{the peak activation memory}{%
  \small
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The sums in bytes:
          \vskip 0.1em
          {\footnotesize
          \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.05}
          \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
            \toprule
            \textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Input} & \textbf{Output}
            & \textbf{Sum} \\
            \midrule
            1 & 27\,648  & 36\,864  & 64\,512 \\
            2 & 36\,864  & 36\,864  & 73\,728 \\
            3 & 36\,864  & 18\,432  & 55\,296 \\
            4 & 18\,432  & 110\,592 & 129\,024 \\
            5 & 110\,592 & 27\,648  & \textbf{138\,240} \\
            \bottomrule
          \end{tabular}}
          \vskip 0.1em
          The peak is at layer 5: 138\,240 bytes, or 135 KB.
    \item The factor is $(160 / 96)^2 = 2.78$. The new peak is
          $138\,240 \cdot 2.78 = 384\,000$ bytes, or 375 KB.
    \item Only the model for $96 \times 96$. Its peak of 135 KB is below
          300 KB. The peak of 375 KB is above it.
    \item In the PSRAM. With the frame buffer of 225 KB in the internal
          RAM, the model for $96 \times 96$ has no space also:
          $225 + 135 = 360$ KB.
  \end{enumerate}
}
