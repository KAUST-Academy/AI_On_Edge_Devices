\begin{frame}{From text to an action}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\scriptsize, >=stealth,
      box/.style={draw=coolgray, rounded corners=2pt, align=center,
                  minimum height=0.9cm, text width=1.8cm}]
    \node[box, fill=boxgray] (u) at (0,0) {``It is too dark in the bedroom.''};
    \node[box, fill=darkcyan!15] (m) at (2.6,0) {Language model};
    \node[box, fill=cardinalred!12, text width=2.5cm] (j) at (5.55,0)
      {\ttfamily \{"device": "lamp", "action": "on", "room": "bedroom"\}};
    \node[box, fill=boxgray] (p) at (8.5,0) {Your code: check, then act};
    \draw[->] (u) -- (m); \draw[->] (m) -- (j); \draw[->] (j) -- (p);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vskip 0.6em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A program cannot use a free text answer. It needs fields with
          known names and known values.
    \item A language model is good at the first step: many ways to say the
          same command become one meaning.
    \item Three methods connect the model to a program: a schema for the
          output, function calls, and retrieval of facts.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{Use the model to understand the text. Use your code to decide
    and to act.}
  \source{Chapters ``SLM: Basic Optimization Techniques'' and ``Experimenting
    with SLMs for IoT\\ Control'' of \emph{Edge AI Engineering}.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ schema
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Structured output with a schema}
\begin{lstlisting}
schema = {"type": "object", "required": ["device", "action", "room"],
  "properties": {
    "device": {"type": "string",
               "enum": ["lamp", "fan", "heater", "door", "pump"]},
    "action": {"type": "string", "enum": ["on", "off", "open", "close"]},
    "room":   {"type": "string",
               "enum": ["kitchen", "bedroom", "garage", "office"]}}}
r = ollama.generate(model="llama3.2:1b", prompt=text, format=schema,
                    options={"temperature": 0})
\end{lstlisting}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The runtime changes the schema into a grammar. At each step it
          allows only the tokens that keep the output valid.
    \item The output is then valid JSON with the correct keys and allowed
          values. The model still selects the values.
    \item llama.cpp: the schema does not go into the prompt. Describe the
          fields in the prompt too.
    \item \code{format="json"} is weaker: valid JSON, but any keys.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{Ollama API documentation: structured outputs. llama.cpp
    documentation: GBNF grammars.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Measured: valid JSON and correct JSON}
  \small
  20 commands for a home controller. The prompt names the three fields and
  their values. Number of answers out of 20:
  \vskip 0.2em
  \footnotesize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.05}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.24\textwidth}L{0.08\textwidth}rrr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Mode} & \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Parses as JSON} &
    \textbf{Valid fields} & \textbf{Correct} \\
    \midrule
    Prompt only & 1B & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
     & 3B & 20 & 20 & 20 \\
    \code{format="json"} & 1B & 20 & 20 & 18 \\
     & 3B & 20 & 20 & 20 \\
    \code{format=schema} & 1B & 20 & 20 & 18 \\
     & 3B & 20 & 20 & 20 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.2em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The 1B model put each answer of the first mode into a Markdown
          code block. A strict parser rejects all 20. Without the block: 20
          valid and 17 correct.
    \item With a format, the 1B model gives 2 wrong values: ``It is too dark
          in the bedroom'' becomes \code{lamp}, \code{off}. ``The office is
          cold'' becomes \code{heater}, \code{off}.
    \item The 3B model is correct 60 times out of 60, and 29 percent
          slower.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{A schema makes the output valid. It does not make it correct.}
  \source{Experiment of this course: Ollama 0.32.6, CPU only,\\ temperature
    0.}
\end{frame}

    \node[box, fill=darkcyan!15, text width=4.0cm] (m2) at (4.85,-1.4)

    \draw[->] (q) -- (m1); \draw[->] (m1) -- (c); \draw[->] (c) -- (f);

  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vskip 0.4em
\begin{lstlisting}
tool = {"type": "function", "function": {"name": "multiply",
  "parameters": {"type": "object", "required": ["a", "b"],
    "properties": {"a": {"type": "integer"}, "b": {"type": "integer"}}}}}
r = ollama.chat(model="llama3.2:1b", messages=msgs, tools=[tool])

\end{lstlisting}
  \small
  The model selects the function and the arguments. Your code runs the
  function. A second call of the model can write the answer as text.
  \source{Ollama API documentation: tool calling. Chapter ``SLM: Basic
    Optimization Techniques''\\ of \emph{Edge AI Engineering}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Retrieval-augmented generation}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\scriptsize, >=stealth,
      box/.style={draw=coolgray, rounded corners=2pt, align=center,
                  minimum height=0.75cm, text width=1.9cm}]
    \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries, anchor=west] at (-1.1,1.25) {Once};
    \node[box, fill=boxgray] (d) at (0,0.5) {Documents};
    \node[box, fill=boxgray] (ch) at (2.6,0.5) {Chunks};
    \node[box, fill=darkcyan!15] (e1) at (5.2,0.5) {Embedding model};
    \node[box, fill=cardinalred!12] (db) at (7.8,0.5) {Vector store};
    \draw[->] (d) -- (ch); \draw[->] (ch) -- (e1); \draw[->] (e1) -- (db);
    \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries, anchor=west] at (-1.1,-0.55) {Each question};
    \node[box, fill=boxgray] (q) at (0,-1.3) {Question};
    \node[box, fill=darkcyan!15] (e2) at (2.6,-1.3) {Embedding model};
    \node[box, fill=cardinalred!12] (k) at (5.2,-1.3) {Top $k$ chunks};
    \node[box, fill=darkcyan!30] (m) at (7.8,-1.3) {Language model};
    \draw[->] (q) -- (e2); \draw[->] (e2) -- (k); \draw[->] (k) -- (m);
    \draw[->] (db) -- node[right] {most similar} (db |- k.north) -- (k.north east);
    \draw[->] (q.south) -- ++(0,-0.35) -| node[pos=0.25, below] {question + chunks} (m.south);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vskip 0.4em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The facts come from your documents at the time of the question.
          The model does not need new training.
    \item An embedding model maps a text to a vector. Similar texts give
          near vectors: compare them with the cosine similarity.
    \item Example: \code{nomic-embed-text}, 137M parameters, vectors of
          768 values. It runs in Ollama on the same board.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{Lewis et al., 2020. Chapter ``SLM: Basic Optimization
    Techniques'' of \emph{Edge AI Engineering}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The cost of retrieval on the board}
  \small
  Each retrieved chunk makes the prompt longer. The prompt rate of the
  board sets the time before the first token.
  \vskip 0.3em
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.34\textwidth}rr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Prompt} & \textbf{Tokens} & \textbf{At 19.46 tokens/s} \\
    \midrule
    The question only & 47 & 2.4 s \\
    The question and 2 facts & 94 & 4.8 s \\
    The question and 12 facts & 309 & 15.9 s \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.4em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item 19.46 tokens/s is the published prompt rate of \code{llama3.2:1b}
          on the Raspberry Pi 5 with a short prompt. The times are
          estimates: measure them in the lab.
    \item Keep the chunks short and $k$ small. Retrieve only what the
          answer needs.
    \item The embedding of the question also takes time, but much less: the
          model is small and the question is short.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{Retrieval trades RAM and prompt time for correct facts.}
  \source{Prompt tokens: the experiment of this course. Rate: lab ``Small
    Language Models''\\ of \emph{Machine Learning Systems}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A language model that controls devices}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\scriptsize, >=stealth,
      box/.style={draw=coolgray, rounded corners=2pt, align=center,
                  minimum height=0.8cm, text width=1.5cm}]
    \node[box, fill=boxgray] (s) at (0,0) {Sensors and a button};
    \node[box, fill=boxgray, text width=2.1cm] (p) at (2.3,0) {Prompt: readings, rules, command};
    \node[box, fill=darkcyan!15] (m) at (4.6,0) {Language model};
    \node[box, fill=cardinalred!12] (c) at (6.6,0) {JSON, then a check};
    \node[box, fill=boxgray, text width=0.9cm] (l) at (8.4,0) {LEDs};
    \draw[->] (s) -- (p); \draw[->] (p) -- (m); \draw[->] (m) -- (c); \draw[->] (c) -- (l);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The chapter connects a temperature sensor, a pressure sensor, a
          button, and three LEDs to the Raspberry Pi 5.
    \item The prompt has the readings and the rules in order of priority:
          the button (an alarm) first, then the temperature, then normal
          operation.
    \item The model answers with a JSON object that names one LED. If the
          program cannot parse the answer, the LEDs keep their state.
    \item A person can also give a command in a natural language, for
          example ``turn on the yellow LED if the temperature is above 20
          degrees''.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The rules of the prompt are a request, not a guarantee. Put the
    safety rules into your code.}
  \source{Chapter ``Experimenting with SLMs for IoT Control'' of \emph{Edge AI
    Engineering}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Guard every action}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.30\textwidth}L{0.60\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Guard} & \textbf{Example} \\
    \midrule
    A list of allowed actions & Only the functions and values of the schema
                                exist \\
    A check of each argument  & The heater accepts 5 to 30 degrees, not 300 \\
    A rule for safety         & The alarm button turns the red LED on, with
                                no model in the path \\
    A person confirms         & Opening a door needs a ``yes'' from the
                                user \\
    A time limit              & No answer in 10 s: keep the state, write a
                                log line \\
    A log                     & Each prompt, answer, and action, with the
                                time \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Never run the text of a model as code or as a shell command.
    \item A document or a web page can contain an instruction, and the
          model can follow it. Your code checks each output.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{The model proposes. The code decides.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Wrong answers}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A language model writes a probable text. A probable text can be
          false, and it sounds as sure as a true one.
    \item Temperature 0 gives the same answer each time. It does not give
          the correct answer.
  \end{itemize}
  \vskip 0.2em
  \footnotesize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.05}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.30\textwidth}L{0.46\textwidth}L{0.14\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Question, no facts} & \textbf{Answer of \code{llama3.2:1b}} &
    \textbf{The fact} \\
    \midrule
    Below which light level does the light panel switch on? & ``\ldots usually
      around 10-20 lux.'' & 3500 lux \\
    At which humidity does the fan start? & ``\ldots when the relative humidity
      falls below 40\%.'' & 70 percent \\
    How long must I hold the RESET button? & ``You don't need to hold any
      buttons \ldots'' & 8 seconds \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.3em
  \small
  Countermeasures: retrieval, tools, a schema, and a test set.
  \keypoint{Measure the accuracy of your application, as for a classifier:
    a test set, a count, and a target.}
  \source{Experiment of this course: Ollama 0.32.6, temperature 0. Chapter
    ``SLM: Basic\\ Optimization Techniques'' of \emph{Edge AI Engineering}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The latency of one request}
  \small
  One question with a tool call, \code{llama3.2:1b}. The token counts are
  measured. The times are estimates for the Raspberry Pi 5 with the
  published rates: 19.46 tokens/s for the prompt, 8.99 for the answer.
  \vskip 0.3em
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.08}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.48\textwidth}rr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Step} & \textbf{Tokens} & \textbf{Time} \\
    \midrule
    Call 1: the prompt with the tool list & 165 & 8.5 s \\
    Call 1: the tool call                 & 26 & 2.9 s \\
    Your function                         &    & 0.0 s \\
    Call 2: the new part of the prompt    & 96 & 4.9 s \\
    Call 2: the answer as text            & 16 & 1.8 s \\
    \midrule
    Total                                 &    & 18.1 s \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.4em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The tool list makes the first prompt long: a question of 5 words
          became 165 tokens.
    \item Call 2 is not always necessary: your program can show the result
          of the function. Then the total is 11.4 s.
  \end{itemize}
  \source{Token counts: experiment of this course. Rates: lab ``Small
    Language\\ Models'' of \emph{Machine Learning Systems}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Heat and power}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item During the generation, all 4 cores of the Raspberry Pi 5 run at
          almost 100 percent. This is the heavy load of Day 9: 7 to 10 W,
          70 to 80 $^\circ$C with the active cooler (published ranges).
    \item With Florence-2, the temperature went above 80 $^\circ$C. A
          board that throttles loses 20 to 30 percent of its rate.
  \end{itemize}
  \vskip 0.3em
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.05}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.62\textwidth}r@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Estimate: one answer of 100 tokens, \code{llama3.2:1b}} &
    \textbf{Value} \\
    \midrule
    Time at 8.99 tokens/s           & 11.1 s \\
    Energy at 7 to 10 W             & 78 to 111 J \\
    Answers from a battery of 3.7 Wh (13\,320 J) & 120 to 171 \\
    The kit chip of Day 9 at 217 mW for 1 hour & 781 J \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.4em
  One hour of the active kit chip uses the energy of 7 to 10 answers.
  \keypoint{A language model is the most expensive way to make a decision on
    the board. Use it where nothing smaller works.}
  \source{Day 9, Part 3. Lab ``Small Language Models'' of \emph{Machine
    Learning Systems}. Guide ``SLMs\\ at the Edge'', section 5. Energy:
    estimates of this course.}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ wrong tool
\begin{frame}{When a language model is the wrong tool}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.32\textwidth}L{0.22\textwidth}L{0.36\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{The task} & \textbf{The tool} & \textbf{Example} \\
    \midrule
    Fixed classes from a sensor or an image & A classifier (Days 1 to 9)
      & A keyword, a gesture, a cup or a bottle \\
    An exact condition & A rule in code & Temperature above 31
      $^\circ$C: open the window \\
    A calculation & Code, or a tool & A sum, a mean, a distance \\
    Free text, many ways to say one thing & A language model
      & A spoken command, a summary of a log \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vskip 0.5em
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A classifier and a rule are fast, cheap, and they give the same
          answer each time. You can test them completely.
    \item A language model is flexible, but slow, large, and sometimes
          wrong.
    \item A good design often combines them: the model reads the text, a
          rule checks the result, and a classifier watches the sensor.
  \end{itemize}
  \keypoint{Fixed function first. A language model only for the part that
    needs language.}
  \source{Chapter ``Classification of AI Applications''\\ of \emph{Edge AI
    Engineering}.}
\end{frame}

\exerciseframe{a classifier, a rule, or a language model?}{%
  \small
  For each task, select a classifier, a rule, or a language model. Give one
  reason, and one number that supports it.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item A door sensor sends ``open'' or ``closed''. Send a message when
          the door is open for more than 5 minutes at night.
    \item A camera at the door of a shop counts the people who come in.
    \item A farmer sends a voice note: ``the pump in the east field makes a
          strange noise since this morning''. Make a maintenance ticket
          with the device, the place, and the problem.
    \item A battery sensor node must detect the word ``stop'' for one year
          on a small battery.
  \end{enumerate}
  For task 3, write the JSON schema of the ticket with three fields.
}

\answerframe{a classifier, a rule, or a language model?}{%
  \small
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item A rule: \code{if door == "open" and minutes > 5 and night}. It
          needs no model, it is exact, and you can test it completely.
    \item A classifier, as a detector of people (Day 8). It runs at many
          frames in each second. A language model needs seconds for each
          image.
    \item A language model: the text is free, and the same problem has many
          forms. Schema: \code{device} (enum of the devices), \code{place}
          (string), \code{problem} (string). One ticket in 10 to 20 s on
          the board is acceptable.
    \item A classifier: keyword spotting on the microcontroller (Day 5).
          The chip of the kit sleeps at 0.79 mW (Day 9). A language model
          needs 7 to 10 W on the Raspberry Pi.
  \end{enumerate}
  \keypoint{The task decides the tool. A language model is one tool among
    four.}
}
